Pan-African Food, Made for the Table

Explore the Pan-African menu at Flavors From Afar in Los Angeles, with dishes inspired by culinary traditions across the African continent.

StartersEntréesSidesDesserts

Menu updated November 3, 2026

Lunch: 11 AM–3 PM · Happy Hour: 3–6 PM · Dinner: 5–10 PM · Brunch: Coming soon.

Starters

Salatet Ful

Tender fava beans tossed with shaved cucumber, cherry tomato, shallot and fresh herbs, finished with lemon and garlic oil.

Taste profileBright, lemony and herbal over a creamy, earthy bean base. Cool and crunchy, with no heat.

The story

Fava beans have fed Egypt since the time of the pharaohs, and ful is still the country's everyday breakfast, ladled from street carts all over Cairo. Salatet ful is its fresh, cool cousin: the same beans, dressed as a salad instead of stewed.

EgyptVegan

Charred Cabbage

Charred cabbage over whipped herb labneh with preserved lemon, feta, chile oil and fresh herbs.

Taste profileSmoky and sweet at the edges, creamy and tangy underneath, with a salty, citrusy lift and gentle chile warmth.

The story

Cabbage is one of the humblest vegetables in the North African kitchen. We char it until the edges turn sweet and smoky, then dress it with the bold, salty flavors of the Maghreb: preserved lemon, tangy labneh and feta.

North AfricaVegetarian

Sambusa

Crisp pastry triangles filled with xawaash-spiced lamb, onion and herbs, or with cabbage, carrot and potato, served with basbaas, a Somali chile sauce.

Taste profileA shatteringly crisp shell around a savory, warmly spiced filling, with a bright chile kick from the basbaas.

The story

Sambusa are a fixture of Somali and East African tables, above all at iftar, when families break the Ramadan fast together. A cousin of the samosa, it traveled the Indian Ocean trade routes. The Somali version is seasoned with xawaash, the warm spice blend at the heart of Somali cooking.

East AfricaVegetarianVegetarian or lamb

Zaalouk

Smoky eggplant and tomato cooked down with garlic, cumin, paprika and olive oil into a soft, spoonable salad.

Taste profileSmoky, savory and gently tangy, with warm cumin and a silky, spreadable texture.

The story

In Morocco, a meal often opens with a spread of cooked salads, and zaalouk is one of the most loved. Eggplant and tomato are cooked down slowly with garlic and spices until soft enough to scoop up with bread.

MoroccoVegan

Akara with Kani Sauce

Golden fritters of ground black-eyed peas, fried until crisp and served with Senegalese kani pepper sauce.

Taste profileCrisp outside, light and fluffy inside, earthy and savory, with a spicy kick from the kani sauce.

The story

Akara, crisp black-eyed pea fritters, are a street-food staple across West Africa, from Lagos breakfast stands to the markets of Dakar. In Senegal they are called accara and come with kani, a fiery pepper sauce. The recipe crossed the Atlantic with enslaved West Africans and lives on in Bahia, Brazil, as acarajé.

West Africa

Entrées

Catch of the Day

The day's freshest fish, prepared with seasonal flavors from across the continent. Ask your server for today's preparation.

Taste profileChanges with the catch and the preparation. Your server will walk you through today's flavors.

The story

Africa's coastlines, from the Atlantic to the Indian Ocean, each have their own way with the day's catch. Our kitchen picks the freshest fish every day and cooks it with flavors from across the continent, so no two days taste quite the same.

Suggested with So Far Out Sparkling

Across Africa

Potjie Oxtail

Oxtail slow-cooked potjie-style in a cast-iron pot with warm spices until rich and tender.

Taste profileDeep, beefy and rich, with warm spices and a meltingly tender texture.

The story

Potjiekos, Afrikaans for “small pot food,” is South Africa's fireside tradition: a three-legged cast-iron pot left to simmer over coals while friends gather around it. Oxtail, loved from the Caribbean to the American South, is made for that kind of patience.

Suggested with Ferme Rouge

South Africa

Mechoui Lamb Shank

Lamb shank slow-roasted in the mechoui style with cumin and warm spices until it falls from the bone.

Taste profileRich and savory, with earthy cumin, warm spice and fall-off-the-bone tenderness.

The story

Mechoui is Morocco's celebration lamb, traditionally a whole lamb roasted for hours over fire or in a clay pit for weddings, Eid and other feasts. The meat is so tender that it is pulled apart by hand at the table.

Suggested with Ferme Rouge

Morocco

Coconut Curry

Coconut curry with shellfish, warm spices, seasonal vegetables, fresh herbs and citrus. Also available as a vegetable curry.

Taste profileCreamy and fragrant, with warm spices, gentle coconut sweetness and a bright citrus finish.

The story

Zanzibar, the spice island off the coast of Tanzania, sits where African, Arab and Indian cooking meet. Its coconut curries, perfumed with the cloves and warm spices the island is famous for, are the taste of the Swahili Coast.

Suggested with Ferme Blanc

TanzaniaVegetable version available

Piri Piri Prawns

Ten prawns roasted in cast iron with fiery piri-piri sauce, charred lemon and fresh herbs.

Taste profileHot and citrusy, with sweet, juicy prawns, a smoky char and fresh herbs.

The story

Piri-piri, the small, fiery African bird's eye chile, became Mozambique's signature after Portuguese traders carried chiles there from the Americas. The sauce built around it now travels the world. Its home is the Mozambican coast, famous for its prawns.

Suggested with Ferme Blanc

Mozambique

Half Chicken

Roasted half chicken seasoned with xawaash and the warming spices of Somali cooking, served with basbaas chile sauce, citrus and fresh herbs.

Taste profileSavory and aromatic, with warm spice, crisp skin and juicy meat, finished with a fresh chile kick.

The story

Xawaash, Somali for “spices,” is the warm blend at the heart of Somali cooking, shaped by centuries of trade across the Indian Ocean. Paired with basbaas, Somalia's beloved chile sauce, it turns a roast chicken into a taste of Mogadishu.

Suggested with Le Gris Rosé

Somalia

Goat Pepper Soup

Tender goat simmered in a peppery Nigerian broth with warming spices, chile and fresh herbs.

Taste profileSpicy, aromatic and warming, with a light but deeply savory broth and tender goat.

The story

In Nigeria, pepper soup is the comfort food of celebrations, cold evenings and long conversations, usually with a cold drink close at hand. Goat is the classic choice, simmered with a fragrant spice blend and plenty of chile.

Suggested with Le Gris Rosé

Nigeria

Koshari

Lentils, rice, chickpeas and pasta layered with spiced tomato sauce, crispy shallots and a toasted cumin vinaigrette.

Taste profileHearty and tangy, with warm cumin, a sweet-sour tomato sauce and crunchy shallots.

The story

Koshari is Egypt's national dish and its favorite street food, a fixture of Cairo since the 19th century. It pairs lentils and rice like India's khichdi, which may have given koshari its name, then adds pasta, chickpeas and a tangy tomato sauce.

Suggested with Ferme Rouge

EgyptVegan

Sides

Jollof Rice

Rice simmered in one pot in a smoky base of tomatoes, peppers and onions until every grain is deeply seasoned.

Taste profileSmoky, savory and gently peppery, with rich tomato depth and tender, separate grains.

The story

Jollof takes its name from the Wolof, or Jolof, Empire of the Senegambia region, and it is the celebration dish of West Africa. Nigeria, Ghana and Senegal each claim the best version, a friendly rivalry known as the jollof wars.

West Africa

Swahili Pilau

Fragrant rice cooked with warm spices, served with kachumbari, a fresh salad of tomato and onion.

Taste profileAromatic and warmly spiced without heat, balanced by the cool, tangy crunch of kachumbari.

The story

Pilau reached the East African coast through centuries of Indian Ocean trade, and Swahili cooks made it their own with warm whole spices. It is the rice of weddings and Eid from Mombasa to Zanzibar, and it is traditionally served with kachumbari.

TanzaniaVegan

Sukuma Wiki

Collard greens braised with tomato and onion until tender.

Taste profileSavory, earthy and mildly tangy, with tender greens and a light tomato sweetness.

The story

In Swahili, sukuma wiki means “push the week” or “stretch the week.” Collard greens are affordable and plentiful, so they carry families from one payday to the next. It is everyday food across Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania, usually eaten with ugali.

KenyaVegan

Sweet Plantains

Ripe plantains fried until caramelized and finished with flaky sea salt.

Taste profileSweet and caramelized, with a soft, creamy center and a salty finish.

The story

Fried ripe plantains go by many names across Africa and its diaspora: dodo in Nigeria, kelewele when spiced in Ghana, alloco in Côte d'Ivoire and maduros in the Caribbean. Wherever they are made, they disappear fast.

Across AfricaVegan

Chapati (2)

Two soft, flaky layered flatbreads, made for tearing and scooping.

Taste profileSoft and chewy, with flaky golden layers and a lightly toasted flavor.

The story

Chapati arrived in East Africa with Indian workers and traders in the late 1800s and became a staple across Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda. The East African version is rolled in layers with oil, which makes it softer and flakier than its Indian ancestor.

East AfricaVegan

Desserts

Orange Blossom Basbousa

Golden semolina cake soaked in fragrant orange-blossom syrup.

Taste profileSweet, floral and moist, with a tender, slightly grainy crumb.

The story

Basbousa is the beloved semolina cake of Egypt and the wider Middle East, baked for holidays, Ramadan evenings and everyday cravings. A soak in syrup keeps it moist; ours is perfumed with orange blossom.

Egypt

Malva Pudding

Warm baked apricot sponge pudding soaked in a buttery cream sauce.

Taste profileRich, sweet and caramelized, with a soft, sticky crumb and a hint of apricot tang.

The story

Malva pudding is South Africa's favorite comfort dessert, with Cape Dutch roots, and is served warm after Sunday lunch. The origin of its name is debated, but its signature is not: an apricot sponge soaked in a warm, buttery cream sauce.

South Africa

Thiakry

Sweet millet folded into creamy, lightly sweetened yogurt.

Taste profileCool, creamy and gently tangy, with a soft, grainy texture and light sweetness.

The story

Thiakry, also spelled chakery, is Senegal's creamy millet dessert and a favorite at family celebrations. Millet is one of Africa's oldest cultivated grains, and here it becomes something cool, soft and comforting.

Senegal

Bissap Granita

West African hibiscus, steeped and shaved into a bright, tart granita.

Taste profileTart, floral and refreshing, with berry-like hibiscus notes and an icy, crystalline texture.

The story

Bissap, a deep-red drink of hibiscus flowers, is a favorite across West Africa. Ghana calls it sobolo, Nigeria calls it zobo, and it is often called Senegal's national drink. We freeze it into an Italian-style granita for a bright finish to the meal.

West Africa

Mahamri & Chai

Swahili coconut and cardamom doughnuts with a cup of spiced East African chai.

Taste profileLight, fluffy and gently sweet, with coconut and cardamom aromas and a warm, spiced tea.

The story

On the Swahili Coast, mornings start with mahamri, pillowy doughnuts made with coconut milk and cardamom, and a cup of spiced chai. You will find them in homes and street stalls from Mombasa to Zanzibar.

East Africa
